Star-Forming Main Sequence

At the moment, I'm working with Prof. Danilo Marchesini on fitting the relation between star formation rate and stellar mass for star-forming galaxies (also known as the Star-Forming Main Sequence!). This relationship is important for many reasons: its scatter The main objective of my project is to constrain the parameters(slope, normalization, and scatter) and high-mass behavior of the relation, since results differ among previous studies. To do this, I'm using a JWST sample of ~70,000 galaxies across a wide mass range and fitting the sequence with the emcee package out to z~9. N-body Simulations

For my "Exoplanets and Their Orbits" course, I did a final project taking a look at the stability of disks in the event of a stellar flyby using REBOUND. I was specifically comparing the fraction of escaped planetesimals from a disk around a single star versus a binary star system. I varied both the initial trajectory distance and velocity of the stellar flyby. To my surprise, I found that the planetesimals around the binary star system were more easily perturbed! The figure shows that result where both panels are showing the fraction of planetesimals lost over time. The different colors represent the varying initial velocities of the flyby. The top panel is the binary system while the bottom is the single star system.

Bulge Radii Estimates

In undergrad, I did an honors thesis on improving measurements of the effective bulge radii for a small sample of early-type galaxies with GALFIT modeling. Previous work had generally used one-component models for galaxies to obtain this radius and obtain the corresponding stellar velocity dispersion, but there are many more structures in a galaxy, so a multi-component approach is necessary.
