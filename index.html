Hi! I'm a 2nd-year student in the Astrophysics M.S. program at Tufts University, and I received my B.S. from Emory University, majoring in Physics and Astronomy with a minor in Math. I'm currently working on constraining the star formation rate - stellar mass relation for star-forming galaxies using JWST data! In the past, I've also done some research on fitting light profiles of early-type galaxies using GALFIT and emission line modeling for AGN outflows (see my projects section for more details).

Here are some projects I've worked on!

At the moment, I'm working with Prof. Danilo Marchesini on fitting the relation between star formation rate (SFR) and stellar mass for star-forming galaxies (also known as the Star-Forming Main Sequence!). I've been using a JWST sample of ~70000 galaxies and fitting the sequence out to z~9. The main goal of my project is to constrain the parameters and shape of the relation since there are differing results in the literature.

For my "Exoplanets and Their Orbits" course, I did a final project taking a look at the stability of disks in the event of a stellar flyby using REBOUND. I was specifically comparing the fraction of escaped planetesimals from a disk around a single star versus a binary star system. I varied both the initial trajectory distance and velocity of the stellar flyby. To my surprise, I found that the planetesimals around the binary star system were more easily perturbed!

In undergrad, I did an honors thesis on improving measurements of the effective bulge radii for a small sample of early-type galaxies with GALFIT modeling. Previous work had generally used one-component models for galaxies to obtain this radius and obtain the corresponding stellar velocity dispersion, but there are many more structures in a galaxy, so a multi-component approach is necessary.
